Part 1: Selfie — The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

In this experiment, I asked a friend of mine to take some photos of me by changing the distance between the camera and me while adjusting the zoom to observe how perspective affects facial proportions. (I literally fought for my life trying not to laugh tho)

Comparing the photos above, we can find that the changes in camera position noticeably affect the perspective and apparent facial proportions. At close range, features closer to the camera, such as nose, appear relatively larger, while features farther away appear smaller. From a greater distance, these depth differences become less pronounced, producing flatter and more natural-looking facial proportions.

Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

Here I photographed the same architectural subject from different distances while changing the focal length.

Near + Wide Angle 1.0x

The perspective is strong, making nearby parts of the scene appear relatively larger while more distant parts seem farther away.

Far + Zoom In 2.0x

The perspective appears more compressed, making the relative depth between different parts of the scene less pronounced.

Part 3: The Dolly Zoom

These two GIFs are from some older footage taken with a drone (DJI Flip). I tried to move the drone backward at a constant speed while zooming in using digital zoom at the same time. As a result, the subject stays nearly the same size on screen while the background appears to stretch and recede dramatically, creating the classic “dolly zoom” or “Vertigo effect.”
